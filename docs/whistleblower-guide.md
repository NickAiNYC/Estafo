# Report suspected fraud in NYC and understand possible awards

**Verified October 27, 2026.** This guide is for someone with specific information about suspected fraud involving New York public funds or Medicaid. It explains reporting routes and when an award may be possible. A provider-spending outlier is a lead for investigation; it does not establish fraud.

## Choose the reporting route

| What you know | Official route | Possible payment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Suspected Medicaid provider fraud | [New York Attorney General Medicaid provider complaint](https://ag.ny.gov/medicaid-fraud-complaint) or [OMIG allegation form](https://apps.omig.ny.gov/bmfa/bmfa.aspx) | An ordinary complaint has no automatic award. Ask a qui tam lawyer before submitting if you may pursue a case. |
| Suspected NYC employee or contractor corruption, or fraud against City funds | [NYC Department of Investigation complaint information](https://portal.311.nyc.gov/article/?kanumber=KA-02418) | A separate NYC False Claims Act process may allow a share of a successful recovery. |
| False claims against federal funds, including some Medicaid claims | Consult a qualified False Claims Act lawyer about a federal qui tam action | A successful relator may receive a portion of the recovery. |
| Suspected federal tax fraud | [IRS whistleblower award claim](https://www.irs.gov/help/submit-a-whistleblower-claim-for-award) | A separate IRS process, generally 15–30% of proceeds collected and attributable to the information when eligible. |

## How an award can arise

- **Federal False Claims Act:** A private person can file a qui tam lawsuit on behalf of the United States. Successful relators typically receive **15–30% of the government's recovery**, subject to the statute and the case. The complaint is filed under seal with a disclosure of material evidence; get legal advice before filing or publicizing allegations. Sources: [DOJ FCA overview](https://www.justice.gov/civil/false-claims-act), [DOJ FY2025 results](https://www.justice.gov/opa/pr/false-claims-act-settlements-and-judgments-exceed-68b-fiscal-year-2025), [DOJ qui tam procedure](https://www.justice.gov/archives/usam/criminal-resource-manual-932-provisions-handling-qui-tam-suits-filed-under-false-claims-act).
- **New York False Claims Act:** State Finance Law § 190 allows qui tam actions over false claims affecting state or local government. If government intervenes or converts the case, the ordinary statutory range is **15–25%**; if it does not and the action succeeds, **25–30%**. Public-disclosure and other bars, contribution, and case facts matter; the statute has exceptions. Source: [current § 190 text](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/STF/190).
- **NYC False Claims Act:** The City says successful citizen plaintiffs may, under certain circumstances, retain **up to 30%** of funds they help recover. The City has its own proposed-complaint procedure; have counsel identify the right route and any overlap with state or federal claims. Source: [NYC Law Department](https://www.nyc.gov/site/law/public-resources/new-york-city-false-claims-act.page).

**A tip is not a qui tam filing.** The New York Attorney General's [Taxpayer Protection complaint form](https://ag.ny.gov/sites/default/files/taxpayer-protection-complaint-form.pdf) says submitting that form does not create a right to share in a recovery. If you may have firsthand, material information, consult counsel promptly about timing, public disclosure, and first-to-file issues before sending it widely.

## Prepare a useful, lawful record

1. Record what you personally observed, dates, entities, payment program, and the basis for each statement. Separate firsthand facts from inferences.
2. Preserve documents you already lawfully possess, with source, date, and an unaltered copy. Do not access systems or patient records without authorization.
3. Check the HHS dataset's [granularity and suppression rules](https://opendata.hhs.gov/datasets/medicaid-provider-spending/). Its rows are monthly aggregates; they cannot identify a patient, individual ride, visit, or death.
4. Ask counsel which federal, state, or city process applies and how to protect confidentiality. Do not publish an unverified provider accusation or upload sensitive evidence to a public repository or an unapproved AI service.

For City employees, the [NYC DOI whistleblower FAQ](https://www.nyc.gov/site/doi/report/faqs.page) identifies specific recipients for City whistleblower-law protection; reporting only inside an agency may not qualify. Ask counsel how that rule applies to your role.

## Other ways to earn from the product

After validating the data and controls, sell **provider-spending research subscriptions**, **custom analytical reports**, or **compliance screening** to law firms, providers, and watchdog organizations. Price the research work itself; do not promise recoveries. New York's [Rules of Professional Conduct](https://www.nycourts.gov/ad3/agc/rules/22NYCRR-Part-1200.pdf) restrict payments for recommending lawyers and fee sharing with nonlawyers, so review any referral or contingent-fee arrangement with New York counsel.
